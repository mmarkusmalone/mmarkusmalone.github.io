More about me

I'm a growth engineer at Secureframe, where I build AI agents and internal tools across departments in the company. I studied Computer Science and Creative Writing at Duke, and I'm drawn to autonomous systems and software that take real work off people's plates while keeping their trust.

How I got here

I started coding in high school by making playful things for friends, like an ASMR meditation app and a cat-themed version of brick breaker. I've loved it ever since.

Work

At Secureframe, I built our internal sales room and its agents. One sorts the sales inbox and routes leads to reps, and others reconcile invoices and chase late payments. I also shipped a CMMC gap assessment tool on cmmc.com, and ran a PostHog analytics workshop for engineering.

As an AI intern at Splash Sports, I built RAG apps and a Slack bug-fixing agent that several teams adopted. It taught me that trust in AI tools is earned as much through design, steady iteration, and humility as through the code itself.

Before that, I was an Undergraduate Teaching Assistant at Duke, co-teaching a 28-person recitation for Design and Analysis of Algorithms. I also tutored Java to small groups for three years.

Python, TypeScript, and Ruby are my main languages, and I've built with Rails, PyTorch, LangChain, LangGraph, and MCP. I've also worked across AWS, Vercel, Salesforce, Slack Apps, PostHog, and more. My daily setup is Claude Code, Codex, Linear, and a local dashboard and harness of my own.

Education

I graduated from Duke University in May 2026 with a B.S. in Computer Science and a minor in Creative Writing. I split my time between computer science courses and liberal arts ones. Learning from both taught me to think rigorously and to get a better sense for the truly important problems to solve with software. Some of my favorite classes were Natural Language Processing, Computational Cultural Analysis, and Existentialism.
