Generalization

October 26, 2025

Written for Wittgenstein and Literary Theory at Duke, Fall 2025.

In §293, Wittgenstein writes, “If I say of myself that it is only from my own case that I know what the word ‘pain’ means—must I not say that of other people too? And how can I generalize one case so irresponsibly?”

I love the question: “And how can I generalize one case so irresponsibly?” For me, it gets at the heart of my previously misunderstood picture of the public/private language tension. I was thinking of cases where someone is very self-centered, and when others share their experiences, they respond with, “Oh, that happened to me too,” and then tell a story that’s not actually relevant (and usually not interesting). In this case, the person “generalizes” their experience irresponsibly, assuming their own limited perspective captures everyone else’s. There’s a tension between how they see the relevance or meaning of an experience and what its shared relevance or meaning actually is. I thought this also applied to pain: when someone shares a painful experience, and the self-centered person tries to equate it with their lesser one, they’re generalizing their private sense of pain onto others. But Wittgenstein’s point is that this tension doesn’t arise from anything inherent in someone’s private sensations; it comes from differences in the public ways we talk about pain.

This also made me curious about people who are not as self-centered: those who can genuinely relate their experiences, or even their lack of experience, to others through language. How is it that someone can grasp and empathize with a kind of pain they’ve never personally felt, a pain they understand only through its public descriptions and expressions? We do it all the time. Hearing others share their suffering moves us, even when we haven’t lived it ourselves. I know that this empathy must arise from the meaning that is publicly rooted, but it still puzzles me because empathy does feel so personal. And the way I have always thought about empathy was as arising from one’s ability to relate to others through one’s own experiences. I think my picture of empathy is flawed in the way that I see it springing from someone responsibly generalizing their own experience, as concretely emanating from a single point within oneself. But it is definitely more rooted in the in-between, and can look very different in different situations. It is something I need to think more about to fully wrap my head around.
