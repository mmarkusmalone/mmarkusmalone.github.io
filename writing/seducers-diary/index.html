Seducer’s Diary

January 26, 2025

Written for Existentialism at Duke, Spring 2025.

I really enjoyed reading The Seducer’s Diary. I think Kierkegaard’s mockery of the life of the aesthetic maintains its relevancy in today’s world. One quote that caught my attention was towards the end of the book:

“I am an esthete, an eroticist, who has grasped the nature and the point of love, who believes in love and knows it from the ground up, and I reserve for myself only the private opinion that no love affair should last more than a half year at most and that any relationship is over as soon as one has enjoyed the ultimate” (Kierkegaard, 93).

Kierkegaard makes a caricature of Johannes and his view of love to make a larger claim about the still-common misconception people have on the subject. While erotic and interesting, Johannes’s experience and enjoyment of love are solely superficial, as shown by the fact that he thinks love affairs should last “half a year at most.” It is in this time frame that an average couple will probably have their most exciting and carefree times, but it doesn’t bring out the ethical and deeper qualities of love. In pursuit of the interesting, the aesthete does not access the more fundamental, true experience of love that is not exciting. Still in today’s society, people hold onto the initial courting moments in any relationship because those moments still draw us in and hold fake meaning. We ask couples how they met and when they knew they liked or loved each other, and watch them enjoy telling the “exciting” story. But we don’t ask what the routine is like when you have been together for ten years, or what the most meaningful aspects of long-lasting relationships are, and these are the aspects of relationships that exist in Kierkegaard’s universal and ethical. Our society today worships some of the same superficial relationship ideals as Kierkegaard’s Johannes the Seducer, and it leads to more unhappiness, as love should not always appeal to the interesting and exciting.
