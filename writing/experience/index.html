Experience

September 27, 2025

Written for Wittgenstein and Literary Theory at Duke, Fall 2025.

This week, I was drawn to the consequences of “experience” in understanding meaning and rules. In §155, Wittgenstein writes:

“So, what I wanted to say was: if he suddenly knew how to go on, if he understood the system, then he may have had a distinctive experience and if he is asked: “What was it? What took place when you suddenly grasped the system?”, perhaps he will describe it much as we described it above —– but for us it is the circumstances under which he had such an experience that warrant him saying in such a case that he understands, that he knows how to go on.”

To me, this is one of the clearest places where experience comes into play. The experience of “getting it,” of suddenly grasping how to go on, arises out of practice, the ability to project a concept, and observation. Privately, it may feel as if that moment is the foundation of understanding, but in fact it does not ground it. The experience is contingent on what comes before: public practices and forms of life. Still, the experience of grasping functions as a genuine sign of understanding, because it marks a point from which someone can recognize their progress and continue inquiry.

This definitely shifted my preconception about the value of experience in understanding a concept. For example, there is a certain “aha moment” that comes with being in romantic love, where one finally “gets it.” And my instinct is to ground the genuine understanding of love in the actual experience of romantic love. But if I am understanding Wittgenstein correctly, then that “aha moment” is simply a moment of projection, where years of public practice with reading, observing, and inquiring about what love means secure the circumstances to have that moment. In §176 and §177, he discusses the “because,” where we filter an experience through the concept of a cause; it is not a natural given. In this case, if the experience of having a deep partner for the first time is filtered and structured through the concept of “falling in love,” then the experience cannot itself secure the understanding of the concept, because it is already influenced by the concept. I am still trying to understand the nuances at play here, and am curious to see how it develops as we continue reading.
