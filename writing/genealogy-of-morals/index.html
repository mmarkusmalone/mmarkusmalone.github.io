Genealogy of Morals

February 9, 2025

Written for Existentialism at Duke, Spring 2025.

In the beginning, I found reading Genealogy of Morals extremely difficult with the shifts in language and allusions I didn’t understand. But as I kept reading, I got used to Nietzsche’s style and his philosophies drew me in to the point where reading it was enjoyable.

One concept I am drawn to is Nietzsche’s idea that resentment in the slave morality is simultaneously a toxic product of our sublimated desires and a useful device to keep us responsible in society today. Nietzsche writes, “The slave’s revolt in morality begins when resentment itself becomes creative and gives birth to values— a resentment experienced by those whom deprived as they are of the proper outlet of action, are forced to obtain their satisfaction in imaginary acts of vengeance” (Nietzsche, 25). The inorganic modern emphasis on the “high road” and self-regulation as a moral accomplishment keeps us “deprived” of our “proper outlet of action.” He argues this sublimation complicates our relationship to our natural desires, making people more obscure to themselves.

Another result of this deprivation is the buildup of resentment towards the world and people around us. I also see his argument for how deeply resentful people hold onto their pain and misfortunes for lifetimes, becoming defined by them and worse off for it. It seems like Nietzsche’s ideal would be someone who does not resent: who forgets, who does not sublimate their desires, who does not blame, who lets things go freely.

I am definitely drawn to that ideal, and see how it can make someone freer and more self-realized. But resentment is such a useful tool in society today that I am not sure I would want to give it up. The resentment I still hold toward someone for continuously letting me down or hurting me motivates me not to act like that, making me a more responsible person. And there is great value in being a responsible person in society beyond the superficial. There is a tension between becoming a self-realized “bird of prey” in the Nietzschean ideal and being a beneficial member of society that I don’t know how to resolve, and that may not be resolvable.
