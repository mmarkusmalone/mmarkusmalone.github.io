How AI Affects Work

March 30, 2026

Something I find interesting is the difference between the messaging in the National Academies article on the future of work and the messaging in news outlets and social media on the subject. I have been following the articles on the mass layoffs in tech, and there has been pushback that the companies are blaming the layoffs on AI when what they are actually doing is overcorrecting for overhiring they did during COVID. These layoffs, along with doomsday articles claiming to tell the future of job loss due to AI, seem to direct attention away from what we should actually be doing, as the National Academies article lays out. I am curious to know if there are ways to help defuse the panic and inform the public more clearly on the state of work as it relates to AI.

AI Companions

April 6, 2026

The Broadbent et al. article was enlightening to me because if someone previously asked me if I was against AI companions, I would instinctually agree. But limiting the scope of use to older people and others who are situationally lonely does give me second thoughts about how the technology can actually be pretty helpful to those who need it. I am wondering if the users will self-select (the truly lonely will seek out these devices, which can stimulate them and help them, providing a social good), or if, as these companions get more popular, the user base can grow beyond the intended audience (people with access to community choose AI companions over real people because they are more agreeable and give them the same effect of a positive social interaction). I think there are a lot of risks with this kind of technology, and I would be interested to see how it can truly be regulated, like what was brought up in the FDA paper.
