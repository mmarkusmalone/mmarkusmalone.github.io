Groundhog Day

April 20, 2025

Written for Existentialism at Duke, Spring 2025.

Watching this movie after a semester of learning about existential theories was such an amazing experience. I had already watched the movie when I was younger, but I did not pick up on any of the philosophical themes then. Now, I am able to see a lot of what I have learned, and it was so cool to see how different my perspective is, how much more I can appreciate.

Specifically, I noticed Kierkegaard’s frameworks show up in the movie a lot. One way is that Phil’s growth matched Kierkegaard’s steps toward self-actualization. In the beginning, Phil just kept on being who he was taught to be, a cynic, and played around instead of making any effort to change. Then, Phil started lying and seducing like the aesthete. He reminded me a lot of Johannes the Seducer, manipulating Rita and Nancy to sleep with him. An interesting nuance within this phase was how I felt like the women were presented as unfree objects. This perception changed when Phil grew into the ethical. Living as the aesthete bore no actual fruit with Rita or for his happiness. He shifted, in response to the joy of being in love, to the ethical, where he practiced altruism and actually earned Rita’s love. In my opinion, Rita’s purity and love motivated him to self-actualize so that he could actually be worthy of her love. What was also interesting here was that his altruism was rewarded by society. He did not have to pull any tricks to get what he wanted. In this way, I feel like it aligns with a sort of humanism where we are deeply motivated by good when we have purpose and love.
